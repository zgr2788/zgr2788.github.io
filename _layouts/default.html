Özgür Yılmaz Beker

I am a CS PhD student at the MorphoLab (Dumitrascu Group), located at the Department of Statistics at Columbia University where I work on computational biology. I am highly fortunate to be advised by Bianca Dumitrascu.

During undergrad, I was introduced to computational biology through a joint project in the Adebali and Mustafaoglu labs, working with bulk RNA-Seq data of the blood-brain barrier, which eventually gave rise to the Brendo resource. Additionally, I interned at the European Bioninformatics Institute (EMBL-EBI) as part of the Papatheodorou Group, where I contributed to the codebase of CATD Snakemake.

After obtaining my BSCS from Sabancı University, I worked as a Graduate Research Assistant as part of the Irving Institute for Cancer Dynamics, kickstarting the Ladder framework for modelling single-cell data obtained from multiple experimental conditions.

Research

I'm interested in all things CompBio, with a particular focus on probabilistic modeling of multi-condition (and soon multi-modal / multi-species) omics data. More recently, I am interested in incorporating computer graphics and biophysical modeling for spatiotemporal processes (e.g. development).
